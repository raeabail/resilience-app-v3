# The Resilience Scorecard

A geospatial decision-support framework for assessing landscape resilience across multiple environmental hazards and translating complex spatial information into actionable conservation, restoration, and planning priorities.

The Resilience Scorecard was developed within the Sentinel Landscapes Partnership to help partners, land managers, military installations, conservation organizations, and decision-makers understand where resilience is strongest, where vulnerabilities overlap, and where strategic action could strengthen long-term landscape function.

🌐 **Website:** https://resilience-scorecard.org/

---

## About the Resilience Scorecard

The Resilience Scorecard integrates environmental, hydrologic, landscape, and hazard-related spatial data to evaluate resilience at the parcel scale.

Rather than relying on a single indicator, the framework combines multiple datasets and metrics to characterize how individual parcels may respond to environmental stressors. Results are standardized into resilience classes that allow patterns to be compared across a landscape and across multiple hazards.

The framework is designed to support questions such as:

- Where are existing resilience anchors worth protecting?
- Where could restoration or improved management strengthen resilience?
- Where do multiple vulnerabilities overlap?
- How can resilience information support conservation prioritization?
- How can landscape resilience contribute to military readiness and compatible land use?
- Where can conservation, planning, and partner priorities align?

---

## Hazard Assessments

The Resilience Scorecard currently supports six hazard categories:

- **Drought**
- **Extreme Heat**
- **Flooding**
- **Land Degradation**
- **Severe Storms**
- **Wildfire**

Hazards are assessed independently using metrics appropriate to the landscape and available data.

Individual hazard scores can then be combined into a composite resilience assessment to reveal broader patterns of resilience and vulnerability.

The framework is intentionally adaptable. Not every landscape requires every hazard, and datasets, metrics, and analytical approaches can be refined to reflect local conditions and partner priorities.

---

## How the Framework Works

The general Resilience Scorecard workflow includes:

1. **Define the Study Area**  
   Establish the landscape, installation, agreement area, watershed, or other geographic area of interest.

2. **Identify Relevant Hazards**  
   Select the environmental hazards most important to the landscape and its partners.

3. **Prepare Spatial Data**  
   Compile and standardize national, state, regional, and local geospatial datasets.

4. **Assess Individual Hazards**  
   Evaluate parcel-level resilience using hazard-specific metrics and analytical criteria.

5. **Standardize Scores**  
   Translate results into a common 1–5 resilience scale.

6. **Build the Composite Assessment**  
   Integrate individual hazard scores to examine multi-hazard resilience.

7. **Interpret Landscape Patterns**  
   Identify resilience anchors, vulnerabilities, restoration opportunities, and areas where conservation or management could strengthen resilience.

8. **Move From Analysis to Action**  
   Use results alongside partner priorities, local knowledge, feasibility, mission needs, and other decision-making criteria.

## Sentinel Landscape Applications

The website currently highlights applications of the Resilience Scorecard across multiple Sentinel Landscapes.

### Camp Ripley Sentinel Landscape | Minnesota

The original application of the Resilience Scorecard evaluated parcel-scale resilience across the Camp Ripley Sentinel Landscape and surrounding REPI Agreement area.

The assessment examined:

- Drought
- Extreme Heat
- Riverine Flooding
- Land Degradation
- Wildfire

Results have supported conservation prioritization and easement planning by helping partners identify parcels where resilience, conservation value, and implementation opportunities align.

### Fort Huachuca Sentinel Landscape | Arizona

The Fort Huachuca application examines resilience across an arid, water-limited landscape where water availability and landscape function influence multiple environmental pressures.

The assessment focuses on:

- Drought
- Extreme Heat
- Wildfire

The analysis highlights the relationship between watershed function, landscape resilience, conservation, and long-term military readiness.

### Avon Park Air Force Range Sentinel Landscape | Florida

The Avon Park application examines resilience across a Central Florida landscape shaped by water, fire, severe weather, working lands, conservation, and military activity.

The assessment focuses on:

- Flooding
- Severe Storms
- Wildfire

Results demonstrate how hydrologic function, natural infrastructure, fire-adapted ecosystems, land management, and development patterns interact to influence resilience.

## Design Principles

The Resilience Scorecard is built around several core principles.

### Parcel-Scale Decision Support

Results are designed to support actionable landscape planning rather than simply characterize regional conditions.

### Multi-Hazard Resilience

Different hazards reveal different dimensions of resilience. Examining them together provides a more complete picture of landscape function.

### Adaptability

The framework can be transferred to new landscapes while allowing hazard selection, datasets, metrics, and analytical criteria to reflect local conditions.

### Decision Support, Not Decision Replacement

Scorecard results are one source of information within a broader decision-making process. Final priorities should also incorporate partner objectives, local knowledge, feasibility, conservation value, military mission needs, and other relevant considerations.

### From Analysis to Action

The ultimate goal is not simply to produce resilience scores. It is to help partners understand where strategic conservation, restoration, management, and planning can strengthen landscape resilience.
